A different way to experience tabletop adventures

Beyond the Tabletop.
Inside the Adventure.

For decades, the most powerful part of Dungeons & Dragons wasn't what we could see on the table. It was what we could see in our minds.

We bring the maps to life. You play the adventure.

DNDPOV brings back the feeling of being inside an adventure, not looking down on it, through immersive first-person exploration with your fellow players and Dungeon Master, while preserving the visceral gameplay of rolling dice and fighting battles at the table.

Modern tabletop adventures have increasingly evolved into a quasi-video-game experience, combining some of the least immersive aspects of tabletop gaming and RPGs.

We strive to combine the most immersive aspects of both.

How did we get so far from the adventure?

Tabletop maps, miniatures and virtual tabletops have made it easier to track where everyone is. But they also ask us to spend more time looking at the adventure from above. A miniature on a grid tells you where your character stands. It doesn't make you feel like you're standing there.

The tabletop view

Useful for distances, positioning and combat. But your perspective is above the action, watching tokens and miniatures move around a board.

Theater of the mind

The DM describes a dark passage, and your imagination builds it. Nothing on the table limits what the corridor might feel like.

The DNDPOV view

Now you are looking through your character's eyes. Turn a corner. See a door. Hear the castle. Decide what to do next.

A world of your own, shared with others

Off in your own dark little corridor.

Imagine your party splitting up inside Castle Ravenloft. Your friends are somewhere else in the castle, but you are alone in a narrow, torchlit passage. The light barely reaches the next doorway. You turn a corner and have no idea what waits beyond it.

You're not staring at your miniature on a map while everyone discusses what your token should do. You're there, exploring at your own pace, completely immersed in the moment.

And because it's multiplayer, the others are exploring too. Your Dungeon Master can see where everyone is, introduce an encounter for the characters who are actually present, and bring the group together—or let them wander apart.

Not a video game. Not another virtual tabletop.

DNDPOV isn't trying to automate the adventure or replace the Dungeon Master. It changes how you experience the space, while keeping the part that makes tabletop roleplaying special: people creating a story together.

See through your character's eyes

Explore halls, staircases, rooms and hidden corners from a first-person perspective instead of moving a top-down token.

Explore together—or separately

Multiplayer gives each character independent movement in a shared environment, with the DM able to track their locations.

Let the DM be the DM

Descriptions, encounters, surprises and storytelling remain part of a human-led adventure. Combat and dice still belong to the players.

Keep imagination in the driver's seat

3D surroundings give everyone a shared sense of place without dictating what characters think, feel, say or choose.

The adventure is waiting

Stop looking down at the dungeon.
Step inside it.

Walk through the adventure. Lose yourself in the atmosphere. Find your own dark corridor. And let your Dungeon Master tell you what happens next.
